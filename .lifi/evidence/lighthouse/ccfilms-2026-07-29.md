# Lighthouse evidence — ccfilms.net

Captured 2026-07-29 to substantiate the performance claim published in the
CC Films case study (`app/src/data/site-cases.ts`). The claim existed with
`evidence: "outcome"` but no stored artifact, so it could not be defended if
questioned. This file is that artifact.

## Method

    npx lighthouse https://ccfilms.net/ --only-categories=performance \
      --form-factor=mobile --screenEmulation.mobile \
      --chrome-flags="--headless=new --no-sandbox"

    npx lighthouse https://ccfilms.net/ --only-categories=performance \
      --preset=desktop --chrome-flags="--headless=new --no-sandbox"

Lighthouse 13.4.1, Chrome headless, from a New York residential connection.
Single run per form factor — Lighthouse varies a point or two between runs, so
treat 99 as "99 or 100 on mobile", not a hard floor.

## Result

| Form factor | Performance | LCP | FCP | TBT | CLS |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Mobile** | **99** | 1.9 s | 1.4 s | 10 ms | 0 |
| Desktop | 100 | 0.4 s | — | — | — |

## What the site says

The case study previously published a bare "100". That is true on desktop and
one point high on mobile. The Frontend Modernization Brief §14 is explicit —
"Mobile, not desktop. Desktop scores are easy and meaningless" — so the
published figure is now the mobile number, with desktop noted in the prose.

Both numbers are real. Nothing was fabricated; the claim was simply unqualified
and unarchived.

## Re-measure when

The claim is dated in the case study. Re-run both commands and update this file
if the site is redeployed, or if the figure is ever challenged.
