# Little Fight NYC public copy contract

Every public page speaks to an owner or working manager. The reader is busy,
not stupid, and should never need a technology dictionary to know what happens
next.

## The promise in plain words

Little Fight helps a small business be found, keep the day moving, cut the
drag, and own the parts that fit. That can mean a website, a repair, a free
second opinion, or focused software. It does not mean selling the same platform
to everyone.

## Required page beats

1. Name the real owner problem.
2. Explain the useful next move in plain language.
3. Show what connects: search or referral, website, customer action,
   follow-up, and the accounts or notes the business keeps.
4. State the boundary. Say when a simpler tool, a current vendor, or no work is
   the right answer.
5. Give one clear action. Call, text, email, or start the free read.

## Claim contract

- Owner-entered numbers are estimates, show their formula, and never promise a
  result.
- External facts have a visible direct source link, a date, and enough context
  to tell a study from a guarantee.
- Client facts name a public source or a dated method. Private work stays
  private; no internal analytics or invented public proof.
- Website work may refer to the 14-day promise only with its written eligibility,
  timing, dependencies, and remedy terms. On-site help is a New York service;
  remote nationwide work is website work unless a written scope says otherwise.

## Voice check

Use short sentences. Prefer "what broke?" to "how may we help?" A small wink
is welcome when it points at the mess, never at the owner: "Five logins. One
customer waiting." Never use generic agency filler, fake urgency, or jargon as
a substitute for an answer.

## AEO / SEO check

The question and answer must be in real readable page text. Metadata, FAQ,
heading, internal link, alt text, and visible copy have to agree. Do not stuff
keywords or promise rankings, traffic, conversions, savings, or AI placement.
